How It Works

Share a problem. See the solution.

A partnership has two parts: you provide a challenge, and later in the semester or academic year you come back to see what students built and tell them what you think.

🎯

1 · Provide a Challenge

Share a real problem your organization faces. We help you shape it into a brief and match it to a course or team.

  • Scope a short brief with us — the problem, the context, what success looks like
  • Share any data, or point to a public dataset that mirrors your problem
  • Introduce the challenge to students and answer their questions
  • Check in with teams along the way, if you'd like
💬

2 · See the Solution

Later in the semester or academic year, students present what they built for you. You see the work and give feedback.

  • See working prototypes and the case students make for them
  • Give direct feedback — the part students remember most
  • Take home ideas and artifacts your team can build on
  • Meet students you may want to hire or keep working with
What We Look For

What makes a good challenge

The best challenges are problems you would pay to solve but haven't gotten to. They don't need to be technical — the most interesting ones are about customers, operations, or decisions.

  1. Real. A problem your organization actually faces today, not a hypothetical.

  2. Scoped. Small enough that a student team can show meaningful progress.

  3. AI-shaped. Somewhere AI could plausibly change how the work gets done.

  4. Data-ready. Your own data, or a public dataset that mirrors the problem.

  5. Owned. A named person who can answer questions and cares about the answer.
