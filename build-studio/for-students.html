Undergraduate Pathway

From designing agents, to designing for people, to building with partners, to the capstone.

Year 1
MIS 201 · Redesigned

Fundamentals of Agentic Information Systems

How organizations use information systems, rebuilt around agents: agents, tools, workflows and data. Students model business processes and design agents that act within them.

Design the agent
Concepts · agents as system components
Year 2
New course

Agentic Design

Designing agentic systems with people built in: oversight and hand-off points, accountability, trust, and how work is reorganized around agents.

Design for people
Sociotechnical design · humans in the loop
Year 3 · Two-semester sequence
Fall · 3 cr

Prototype Studio I

AI Product Discovery & Prototyping

Partners bring real problems. Teams run requirements and design sprints, then build and evaluate working AI prototypes. Partner check-in at semester end.

Spring · 3 cr

Prototype Studio II

AI Product Deployment & Evaluation

The same teams take their prototypes into a pilot with the partner: hardening, red-teaming, governance and handoff, ending with a final presentation to partners.

Practice · real partners, real users
Year 4
Capstone

Senior Design

Year-long capstone. Students arrive having completed a full design-build-deploy cycle with a real partner, ready to lead larger, more independent agentic system projects.

Lead the build
Integration · independent capstone

Build Studio in Action · Summer 2026

If they build it, they will learn.

The first summer session of Build Studio ran from June through August 2026. Four student teams, mostly from engineering and computer science, met every Monday for a full-day workshop. Each week ended with an industry guest, such as a venture capitalist, a startup founder, a patent attorney, or a product leader, who heard project updates and gave feedback. The summer closed with a public Open Studio, where teams presented to industry judges.

Build Studio will be a central part of the School of Business’s new undergraduate major in Business and AI, so students graduate with a portfolio of things they have actually built.

“The Build Studio gave me a hands-on project with plenty of creative freedom…” Ted Janaqi · Mechanical Engineering · AI Coworker Drone team

Graduate Spotlight

From capstone idea to published research.

Dr. Kenneth Kern spent more than 40 years as an oncology surgeon and cancer drug developer, and had already studied AI at MIT, Stanford, and Harvard Business School. In the Management of AI graduate certificate, his idea for TrialTriage grew class by class, from a first concept to a minimum viable product in the capstone.

TrialTriage is an agentic AI tool that prescreens patients for Phase I cancer trials. When a case is ambiguous, it emails the physician right away to ask for the missing data, then reclassifies the patient as soon as the reply arrives. The proof-of-concept study, run on synthetic cases, is now published in JMIR Formative Research.

“Without Stevens… training in AI I would have never even conceived of this project.” Kenneth Kern, MD, MPH, MS · Management of AI certificate graduate
