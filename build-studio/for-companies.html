Two Ways to Engage

Challenge Day & Demo Day

Come to one or both. Most partners start by bringing a challenge, then return to judge what teams built with it.

🎯

Challenge Day

Companies present real business challenges to student teams. Teams pick a problem, ask questions, and start building.

  • Pitch your challenge in about five minutes
  • Answer team questions and share any context, data, or constraints
  • Use your own brief and data, or a public dataset that mirrors your problem
  • Stay available for an optional check-in while teams build
⚖️

Demo Day

Teams demo working products and make the business case. Practitioner judges score the work and pick the winners.

  • Watch short live demos and pitches
  • Score teams on value, feasibility, use of AI, and the case they make
  • Give direct feedback — the part students remember most
  • Meet builders you may want to hire or keep working with

What We Look For

What makes a good challenge

The best challenges are problems you would pay to solve but haven't gotten to. They don't need to be technical — the most interesting ones are about customers, operations, or decisions.

  1. Real. A problem your organization actually faces today, not a hypothetical.

  2. Scoped. Small enough that a student team can show meaningful progress.

  3. AI-shaped. Somewhere AI could plausibly change how the work gets done.

  4. Data-ready. Your own data, or a public dataset that mirrors the problem.

  5. Owned. A named person who can answer questions and cares about the answer.
